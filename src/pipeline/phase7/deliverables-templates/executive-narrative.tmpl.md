## The Story in One Sentence

> **{{storyOneLiner}}**

---

## 1. The Setup

{{setup}}

The world the buyer / executive lives in today, the constraint they feel daily, the cost of staying still. Open with a real, observed fact — not an abstraction.

## 2. The Tension

{{tension}}

The gap between what the world demands and what the current operating posture can deliver. The compounding cost of inaction. What competitors are doing. What regulators are signalling. What customers are starting to expect. Why "wait and see" is itself a decision, and an expensive one.

## 3. The Insight

{{insight}}

The non-obvious observation that reframes the problem. Often this is a unit-economics insight — that a per-unit lever, applied across the right scope, scales to a number that changes the conversation. State the insight as a single, sharp claim.

## 4. The Move

{{move}}

The recommendation, but as a story beat — not a list of bullets. What we are doing first, what we are proving, what we are scaling, what stage-gate disciplines us. Show the shape of the bet: small first, then bigger, then biggest, with measured run-rate at each gate.

## 5. The Numbers

{{fcvBlock}}

{{numbersNarrative}}

The numbers, told as a sequence: what we invest, what we capture, when we break even, what the 5-year picture looks like. Always traceable back to the unit-economics manifest. Always presented as ranges where ranges are honest.

## 6. The Risks We Take Seriously

{{risksTakenSeriously}}

The risks we believe could actually break the case — written in plain language, not corporate-speak. For each, the specific signal that would tell us reality has drifted, and the specific action we would take in response.

## 7. The Reversibility

{{reversibility}}

What we can stop, when, and at what cost. The reader should leave knowing this is not an irreversible bet — there are decision points along the way where we can re-decide, with new evidence, and have already paid for the option to do so.

## 8. The Choice

{{choice}}

The decision the reader is being asked to make, in one paragraph. What approval looks like. What deferral looks like. What no-go looks like. The cost of each — including the cost of deferring, which is rarely zero.
